Non-pharmacological interventions for reducing wandering behavior
in people with dementia: a systematic review and network meta-analysis

Population: People with any type of dementia (any setting: community, nursing home, hospital ward)
Interventions (non-pharmacological, candidate network nodes):
  - Environmental modification (visual barriers, camouflaged exits, floor grids, signage, wandering paths)
  - Assistive / monitoring technology (GPS tracking, door alarms, bed/chair exit sensors)
  - Physical activity / structured walking programs
  - Sensory-based therapy (music, multisensory stimulation, aromatherapy, light therapy)
  - Psychosocial / behavioral (person-centred care, structured activities, doll therapy, reminiscence)
  - Caregiver education / training
Comparator: Usual care, attention control, or another non-pharmacological intervention
Outcomes:
  Primary: Frequency or severity of wandering (e.g., NPI aberrant motor behavior subscale, CMAI wandering items, RAWS)
  Secondary:
    - Getting-lost / elopement / exit-seeking events
    - Falls
    - Agitation (CMAI total)
    - Caregiver burden (ZBI)
    - Quality of life (QoL-AD)
    - Physical restraint use
Study designs: RCTs (incl. cluster and crossover). Quasi-experimental studies only if Plan B is triggered (see 00_overview/feasibility.md).
Target journal: Ageing Research Reviews (backup: International Journal of Nursing Studies, Geriatric Nursing)
